Postcards from Saturn.

When Ari looked out of the station window, Saturn seemed to be wearing one enormous silver ring. Ari drew it on a postcard for Grandad. Then a pilot tapped the glass. Look a little longer, she said.

The station turned. The ring separated into bands, with dark gaps between them. Some bands looked pale. Others looked almost gold. From far away they seemed as smooth as ribbon. Up close they were made of countless pieces, mostly ice, moving around the planet together.

Ari rubbed out the smooth line and filled the postcard with dots. On the back Ari wrote, It looks like a ring the way a beach looks like one thing, until you pick up a handful of sand.

The message took much longer to reach Earth than a wave across a room. Ari waited. At last Grandad's reply came. I walked down to the beach today, it said. I thought of you looking at a different kind of shore.

Ari put the postcard beside the window and watched the pieces of ice catch the light.
