The Clockwork Sparrow.

Every creature in Ada's workshop knew exactly what to do. The brass dog fetched a screw at nine. The silver frog jumped at ten. The clockwork sparrow flew to the windowsill every hour on the hour.

One morning a real sparrow landed outside. It did not wait for the hour. It hopped sideways, shook its feathers and disappeared behind a chimney.

The clockwork sparrow watched for it all afternoon. At four, instead of returning to its perch, it flew after the little bird. Ada followed the sound of tiny gears down the lane and found both sparrows beside a puddle. One was bathing. The other was trying to do the same, rather noisily.

Ada rescued her sparrow from the water and dried its springs. She opened its back to see what had gone wrong. Nothing had. The bird had simply found something it liked better than keeping time.

So Ada removed the little chime that called it home on the hour. The next morning, it left when it felt ready. At sunset, it came back with a piece of blue ribbon for her.
