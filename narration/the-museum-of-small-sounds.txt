The Museum of Small Sounds.

The smallest museum in town had no pictures on its walls. Instead it had listening horns. June put her ear to the first one and heard a pencil finish a letter. In the next she heard a scarf brush against a coat.

The curator handed her an empty box. We are missing a sound for the end of the hall, he said. Can you find one?

June brought back a bus horn. The box was too small. She brought back thunder, which made the lid bounce. At last she sat beside her dad on the museum steps and told him she had run out of ideas. He pulled a tangerine from his pocket and began to peel it.

There it was. A tiny tearing sound, followed by the quiet tap of a piece of peel on the step. June opened the box.

The next day, a visitor listened to her exhibit for a very long time. Then she smiled. My dad used to bring me those too, she said. June decided that small sounds could hold quite large things.
