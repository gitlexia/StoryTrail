The Lantern Whale.

On the smallest boat in the harbour, Nia kept a lamp in a blue jar. Every evening she hung it from the mast to show the other boats where the harbour began. But one evening the fog was so thick that Nia could hardly see her own boots.

Beyond the breakwater, a bell rang three times. A fishing boat was looking for the harbour. Nia reached for her lamp, and a gust put it out.

Then a glow rose out of the water. It belonged to a whale, with a little lantern tangled in an old rope above its fin. Nia untied the rope. The whale rolled over to let her take the lantern, then waited.

Nia held the light high. The whale swam ahead, and she followed in her little boat. Together they made a moving path of gold. The fishing boat rang its bell again, this time just once. It had seen them.

At dawn the whale slipped away. That evening Nia hung two lights from her mast. One was for the boats. The other shone all the way out to sea.
